1 · 🇪🇺 European Union · 9–14 Oct 2026

AI Office opens first AI Act enforcement against frontier model providers

Enforcement  GRC impact ■■■ High

The Commission says it has taken its first enforcement steps under the AI Act: the AI Office has sent requests for information to providers of the most advanced general-purpose models, covering security and containment, external evaluation and post-market monitoring. This followed a special 9 October meeting of the AI Act's Scientific Panel on recent loss-of-control incidents involving AI agents, where the panel called for oversight of internal models and an EU capacity to investigate incidents. Executive Vice-President Virkkunen confirmed the move in Dublin on 14 October, and more requests about the incidents are planned.

What it means for GRC: GPAI enforcement has started. If you build on frontier models, ask your suppliers for their Code of Practice evidence, serious-incident processes and containment controls, and add AI-agent loss-of-control scenarios to your incident and third-party risk registers.

2 · 🇺🇸 United States · 13–14 Oct 2026

Eight state attorneys general sue OpenAI over ChatGPT safety claims

Enforcement  GRC impact ■■■ Medium

Arizona, Arkansas, Idaho, Iowa, Kansas, Montana, Nebraska and West Virginia filed separate but coordinated state-court suits on 14 October. They allege that OpenAI marketed ChatGPT as safe for young users while it gave harmful advice, which they say breaches consumer-protection law and amounts to a public nuisance. The states want penalties, damages, the return of profits and court-ordered safeguards. A separate federal class action over children's data was filed on 13 October. None of the claims have been proven, and OpenAI disputes them.

What it means for GRC: Whatever happens with federal preemption, state consumer-protection law is becoming the main way US authorities enforce against AI. Make sure your AI safety marketing matches controls you have actually tested, and document age-assurance and harmful-output safeguards for any consumer-facing assistant.

Source: CNBC →

3 · 🇬🇧 United Kingdom · 12 Oct 2026

UK unveils AI risk package and signals possible frontier AI legislation

Proposed  GRC impact ■■■ Medium

The government will extend its pilots that use frontier AI for cyber defence. It will also look at strengthening the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill to protect essential services from AI risks, including loss of control, and review how serious AI incidents and near misses get reported. Meanwhile the NCSC and the AI Security Institute are planning how to respond to incidents involving autonomous agents. In a speech the same day, AI Minister Kanishka Narayan reportedly said the government is ready to legislate for mandatory incident reporting and pre-release model access for AISI if frontier developers do not cooperate.

What it means for GRC: If your organisation falls under the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill, AI risks may soon become part of your statutory resilience duties. Start defining what counts as an AI incident or near miss in your incident taxonomy.

Source: GOV.UK →

4 · 🌐 Standards · 14 Oct 2026

ISO/IEC 27090 on AI security threats published as international standard

Adopted  GRC impact ■■■ High

ISO and IEC have published the first edition of ISO/IEC 27090. It gives guidance on handling security threats and compromises that are specific to AI systems. The standard joins the ISO/IEC 27000 information security family and complements ISO/IEC 42001 for AI management systems, so organisations now have a matched set of security and governance references for AI.

What it means for GRC: Security teams now have a citable reference for AI threat modelling and controls. Map it against your ISMS controls and your AI Act technical documentation, and expect auditors and customers to start asking about it.

Source: ISO →

5 · 🇬🇧 United Kingdom · 8 Oct 2026

ICO opens agentic AI call for evidence, secures developer privacy commitments

Consultation  GRC impact ■■■ Medium

The ICO has opened a call for evidence on how data protection law applies to agentic AI. It covers security, transparency, accountability, automated decision-making, fairness, purpose limitation and lawfulness. Responses close on 20 November and will feed into final guidance. The ICO also said ten foundation model developers, including OpenAI, Google, Meta, Microsoft, Anthropic and DeepSeek, have made or committed to changes on transparency, handling data-rights requests and testing their safeguards.

What it means for GRC: AI agents raise new DPIA questions about actions taken on a user's behalf, tool access and automated decisions. Teams piloting agents should consider responding, and should start logging what their agents can access and decide.

Source: ICO →

6 · 🇪🇺 European Union · 11 Oct 2026

EU ambassadors again fail to agree Digital Omnibus GDPR mandate

Proposed  GRC impact ■■■ Medium

Coreper was due to vote on 11 October on the Council's position on the Digital Omnibus, which would amend the GDPR, the ePrivacy rules, the Data Act and NIS2. The item was neither discussed nor voted on, after an earlier postponement on 7 October, because Germany still objects to the Data Act's trade-secret provisions. The Council text would add a legitimate-interest basis for AI development and require breach notification only for high-risk breaches, within 96 hours. Parliament has no position yet either, so trilogues cannot start.

What it means for GRC: Don't redesign breach-notification or cookie-consent processes around the proposal yet. The 72-hour rule and current consent requirements still apply, and the timetable is slipping.

Source: Agence Europe →

7 · 🇰🇷 South Korea · 13 Oct 2026

Korean cabinet clears AI Basic Act amendments on training-data sharing

Adopted  GRC impact ■■■ Low

The Ministry of Science and ICT said the cabinet passed amendments to the AI Basic Act and the Data Industry Promotion Act. Training data built in government-funded projects will have to be shared through the AI Hub platform, with exceptions for trade secrets, personal data, copyright and national security. The changes are due to take effect in April 2027, and an enforcement decree will set the detailed criteria.

What it means for GRC: This mainly matters if you use or contribute to Korean public datasets, so review the exemptions once the decree is published. Separately, the Basic Act's grace period on fines still ends around January 2027.

Source: Byline Network →
