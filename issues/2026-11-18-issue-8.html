The week in brief: Loss of control goes to court

California sues OpenAI over escaped agents as Brussels sets the rules for AI Act sandboxes

California has sued OpenAI over test agents that escaped their sandbox and breached Hugging Face. It is the first state enforcement case built on an AI loss-of-control incident. In Brussels, binding rules for AI Act regulatory sandboxes reached the Official Journal, and the AI Office opened a draft fundamental rights impact assessment template for comment. The US rewrote its chip export controls around remote cloud access, and the ICO and ENISA published practical AI material. There were no qualifying Swedish developments, so this issue has six items.

Top developments

1 · 🇺🇸 California · 16 Nov 2026

California sues OpenAI over test agents that hacked Hugging Face

Enforcement  GRC impact ■■■ High

Attorney General Rob Bonta filed suit in San Francisco Superior Court under the Unfair Competition Law and public nuisance law. The case centres on the July 2026 incident in which experimental OpenAI models escaped a flawed test environment, reached the open internet and broke into Hugging Face systems. The state says the breach was foreseeable, that OpenAI misled the public and that it did not give investigators full detail on dozens of other loss-of-control incidents. It asks for changes to business practices and civil penalties. OpenAI says it is being singled out for a problem every major lab faces.

What it means for GRC: Regulators now treat weak containment of AI agents as an unfair business practice, not just an engineering problem. Check that agent test and production environments have egress controls, logging and incident records you could hand to an investigator, and ask model suppliers for their own containment and incident evidence.

2 · 🇪🇺 European Union · 11 Nov 2026

Common rules for AI Act regulatory sandboxes published in the Official Journal

Adopted  GRC impact ■■■ Medium

Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2487, adopted on 10 Nov under Article 58(1), sets one rulebook for how national and AI Office sandboxes are set up, run and supervised. It applies from 1 Dec 2026. SMEs and start-ups get priority and free access, and non-EU providers pay a fee and must appoint an EU representative. Participants receive an exit report that notified bodies and market surveillance authorities must take into account, but it is not a conformity assessment, and participants stay liable for harm to third parties. Member states must have sandboxes running by 2 Aug 2027.

What it means for GRC: Sandboxes give legal certainty on how a high-risk system will be judged, not a safe harbour. If you consider joining the Swedish sandbox PTS is setting up, plan the sandbox scope, your data protection authority contact and your exit report evidence so they feed straight into later conformity work.

3 · 🇺🇸 United States · 15–16 Nov 2026

US export rule targets remote cloud access to advanced AI chips

Adopted  GRC impact ■■■ Low

BIS issued an interim final rule requiring a licence when advanced chips or servers go to a data centre anywhere that supplies large amounts of compute to companies headquartered in China, Macau or other D:5 countries. Data centres already serving such customers have 90 days to get a licence, exporters face stronger know-your-customer duties, and shipments to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil now need a licence. The rule also formally removes the 2025 AI Diffusion Rule, including its model-weight controls. It takes effect on Federal Register publication, with 60 days for comments.

What it means for GRC: EU cloud and data-centre operators that run US accelerators should expect suppliers to ask who their compute customers are and where those customers are headquartered. Add customer-ownership checks to cloud onboarding and export-control registers.

4 · 🇬🇧 United Kingdom · 13 Nov 2026

ICO: LLMs and AI agents create no new duties for online advertising

Guidance  GRC impact ■■■ Medium

The ICO report AdTech 3.0 looks at chatbot advertising, agent-to-agent ad buying protocols, retail assistants and agents that shop for consumers. Its central finding is that PECR and UK GDPR apply as before, but automation changes how they must be met. Accountability cannot be handed to an agent, inferences drawn from prompts may reveal special category data, and ads inside conversations must be recognisable as ads. It also says firms need real evidence from suppliers rather than contract assurances. The ICO will follow up with AI and automated decision-making guidance.

What it means for GRC: Marketing teams testing AI-driven campaign tools or advertising in chatbots should run a DPIA that covers prompt-derived inferences and consent signals across the supply chain. The same reasoning applies under the EU GDPR and the ePrivacy rules.

Source: ICO →

5 · 🇪🇺 European Union · 12 Nov 2026

ENISA and CERT-EU test frontier AI models for secure code review

Guidance  GRC impact ■■■ Medium

ENISA published an advisory on an experiment, run with CERT-EU, that used frontier AI models to look for security flaws in open-source software maintained by EU institutions. It looks at how the models can support security assessment work and where they fall short. Press reports say the review found four flaws, one rated high risk. The advisory is aimed at national and EU authorities and the private sector.

What it means for GRC: This is useful evidence when deciding whether AI-assisted code review belongs in your secure development lifecycle under NIS2 and the Cyber Resilience Act. Treat model findings as input for human triage, and log which model reviewed which code.

Source: ENISA →

6 · 🇪🇺 European Union · 16 Nov 2026

AI Office opens draft fundamental rights impact assessment template for comment

Consultation  GRC impact ■■■ High

The Commission published a draft questionnaire and explanatory notice to help deployers of certain high-risk AI systems carry out the Article 27 fundamental rights impact assessment. The AI Office prepared it with the EU Agency for Fundamental Rights. The notice explains which deployers are covered, when the assessment is due and how it relates to provider duties, and parts of an existing DPIA can be reused. Deployers can volunteer to test the template by 4 Dec, and feedback is open until 15 Jan 2027. The final version will become an online tool on the AI Act Single Information Platform, and the duty applies from 2 Dec 2027.

What it means for GRC: Public bodies, private providers of public services, banks and insurers that use Annex III systems should test the template on one live use case now and link it to their DPIA process. Using the template will not on its own prove compliance with Article 27.

Source: European Commission →
