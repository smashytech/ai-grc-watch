1 · 🇺🇸 New York · 28 Oct 2026

New York bans risky companion-chatbot features for minors

Adopted  GRC impact ■■■ Medium

Governor Hochul signed S.9051B/A.10379C. Operators may not offer minors, or users not confirmed as adults, chatbot features that imitate a real person, claim a relationship with the child, encourage self-harm, secrecy or isolation, or produce sexual content. Providers must offer more than one age-check method, at least one not relying only on government ID, and must delete age data after use. The Attorney General can seek up to $25,000 per violation and order the destruction of unlawfully collected data and of models trained on it. IAPP reports the law takes effect on 1 Jan 2027.

What it means for GRC: If you offer conversational AI to consumers who may be in New York, plan age assurance and a feature review before January. The power to order destruction of models trained on unlawful data is a new remedy to add to training-data and data-lineage risk registers.

2 · 🇺🇸 United States · 26 Oct 2026

FTC escalates AI safety probe and demands testimony from Anthropic executives

Enforcement  GRC impact ■■■ Medium

Axios reported, citing an FTC spokesperson, that the agency has demanded testimony from Anthropic executives in its consumer-harm investigation. This follows the document demands sent to Anthropic and OpenAI about two weeks earlier (Issue 3, item 1). The demand came on the day Anthropic filed for its IPO, and its S-1 warns that the probe could lead to significant costs and changes to model design. The FTC has not named the executives or said whether OpenAI received a similar demand, and no wrongdoing has been found.

What it means for GRC: The probe has moved from documents to sworn testimony, so findings on how labs handle agent incidents are getting closer. Keep the case in third-party risk files for services built on these models, and ask providers how they would pass any required model changes on to customers.

3 · 🇬🇧 United Kingdom · 26 Oct 2026

Bank of England–FCA survey: AI agents widespread, but autonomy tightly limited

Guidance  GRC impact ■■■ Medium

The fourth joint survey, of 196 firms, found that 86% use AI and 80% have AI agents in deployment, but only 2% let agents act with broad autonomy. Most agents recommend actions that people then carry out. Third-party systems make up 53% of high-materiality AI systems, and the three largest cloud providers account for 79% of reported relationships. Almost all firms have AI policies, but only about half monitor systems after deployment, and oversight of vendor models was the most cited governance challenge.

What it means for GRC: Use the figures as a benchmark for your own AI governance, especially post-deployment monitoring and vendor model risk. EU financial entities can map the same concentration and exit-plan questions onto DORA's ICT third-party requirements.

Source: Bank of England →

4 · 🇬🇧 United Kingdom · 26 Oct 2026

Lords defeat government and demand review of AI Security Institute powers

Proposed  GRC impact ■■■ Medium

At Report stage of the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill, peers voted 202 to 155 for a cross-bench amendment. It requires a review, within a year of Royal Assent, of whether AISI should be able to audit frontier AI before deployment, set standards, inspect and recommend suspension. An amendment creating an incident-reporting duty for frontier developers was rejected 95 to 165. A government amendment, agreed without a vote, lets regulations give the Secretary of State inspection, information and penalty powers where AI agents are involved in a network compromise. Third Reading is listed for today, 2 Nov, after which the Commons considers the Lords changes.

What it means for GRC: Statutory frontier AI rules in the UK are still a review away, not law. The AI-agent powers matter sooner: UK operators of essential services could face inspections and information demands after agent-driven incidents, so include agents in NIS-style incident scenarios.

Source: UK Parliament →

5 · 🇺🇸 New York · 29 Oct 2026

New York lawmakers propose ‘RAISE Act 2.0’ with licensed frontier auditors

Proposed  GRC impact ■■■ Low

Senator Gounardes and Assemblymember Wright introduced S.10731 to toughen the RAISE Act before it takes effect on 1 Jan. Developers spending over $1bn on AI within three years would need annual audits by state-licensed frontier examiners, report sharp increases in risk within 72 hours, and file quarterly reports on automated AI research and compute use. A new state office could set binding safety standards and cap training compute, and fines could reach $1m a day for a first violation.

What it means for GRC: Only the largest developers would be covered, but the bill shows where frontier-model assurance is heading: licensed third-party audits and near-miss reporting. Watch it as a template for statutory AI audit regimes.
