The week in brief: Enforcers pick AI targets

EU privacy regulators turn to AI hiring tools as Article 50 guidance lands in the Official Journal

Europe's data protection authorities chose automated and AI-driven recruitment decisions as their joint enforcement topic for 2027, and the Commission's Article 50 transparency guidelines appeared in the Official Journal less than three weeks before the 2 December marking deadline. New York opened registration for frontier AI developers, the ICO set out how to handle AI-written access requests, and Brussels proposed a Digital Fairness Act. NIST and OWASP added agentic AI security material. There were no qualifying Swedish developments.

1 · 🇪🇺 European Union · 11 Nov 2026

EDPB picks AI-driven hiring decisions for its 2027 coordinated enforcement

Enforcement  GRC impact ■■■ High

At its November plenary the EDPB chose automated decision-making in recruitment, including AI-driven candidate screening, as the topic of its sixth Coordinated Enforcement Framework action. Participating authorities will examine how employers respect the right not to be subject to solely automated decisions and the related right of access. DPAs opt in over the coming weeks and the action itself starts during 2027. The same plenary welcomed a new Korean right to refuse automated decisions in its statement on the Korea adequacy review.

What it means for GRC: Inventory CV-screening and candidate-ranking tools now, document where a human genuinely makes the decision, and check you can explain the logic in an access request. If IMY joins, Swedish employers and HR-tech vendors could receive questionnaires in 2027, a year before the same tools become Annex III high-risk under the AI Act.

2 · 🇺🇸 New York · 10 Nov 2026

New York opens RAISE Act registration portal for frontier AI developers

Adopted  GRC impact ■■■ Low

The new DIGIT office within the Department of Financial Services launched the RAISE Portal. Large frontier developers (models trained with more than 10^26 operations, group revenue above $500m) must file a disclosure statement there and keep it current to operate in New York from 1 Jan 2027, when the Act takes effect. The same account will carry 72-hour critical safety incident reports, quarterly catastrophic-risk summaries and assessment payments, and smaller frontier developers are urged to register early so they can report incidents quickly.

What it means for GRC: Only a few labs are covered directly, but their published safety frameworks and transparency reports arrive from January. Add them to the due-diligence evidence you hold on the foundation models your organisation relies on.

Source: NY Department of Financial Services →

3 · 🇪🇺 European Union · 12 Nov 2026

Article 50 transparency guidelines published in the Official Journal

Guidance  GRC impact ■■■ Medium

The Commission's non-binding guidelines on AI Act transparency duties, first released on 20 July and formally adopted on 5 Nov, appeared in the OJ as C/2026/6005. They cover chatbot disclosure, machine-readable marking and detection of synthetic content, deepfake labelling and the exception for AI-generated public-interest text, which requires substantive human review and a publicly identifiable responsible person. Deployers cannot rely on hidden watermarks alone to meet the visible deepfake disclosure duty.

What it means for GRC: Use the OJ text as the reference version for Article 50 gap analyses, and confirm that generative systems already on the market mark their outputs before 2 Dec. Test any “human in the loop” claim for AI-written content against the stricter review standard set out here.

Source: EU Publications Office →

4 · 🇬🇧 United Kingdom · 11 Nov 2026

ICO publishes guidance on handling AI-generated subject access requests

Guidance  GRC impact ■■■ Medium

The ICO says a SAR stays valid if AI drafted it, or if it cites non-existent laws or contains other hallucinated details, as long as the person clearly wants their data. The usual tools still apply: asking for clarification, extending the deadline for genuinely complex requests and refusing manifestly unfounded or excessive ones with a documented justification. It also warns that AI can be used to fake ID or letters of authority, so contact details and authority should be verified.

What it means for GRC: Update DSAR playbooks for longer, AI-written requests, record how you set the scope of each search, and add a verification step for authority documents that may be AI-generated. EU teams face the same trend under GDPR Art. 15.

Source: ICO →

5 · 🌐 Standards · 10 Nov 2026

OWASP releases version 2 of its Securing Agentic Applications guide

Guidance  GRC impact ■■■ Low

The OWASP GenAI Security Project published an updated builder's and defender's guide for agentic AI. It covers secure architectures, identity and access controls, runtime protection, supply-chain security and assurance testing for single- and multi-agent systems. It is a free community resource rather than a formal standard.

What it means for GRC: A practical control set to map against ISO/IEC 42001 Annex A and NIS2 risk measures when reviewing agent deployments, and a ready source of design-review criteria for agent projects.

Source: OWASP GenAI Security Project →

6 · 🇪🇺 European Union · 11 Nov 2026

Commission proposes Digital Fairness Act on dark patterns and addictive design

Proposed  GRC impact ■■■ Medium

The Commission adopted a Digital Fairness Act and a Simplification Directive as part of a consumer package. The DFA targets dark patterns, addictive design, personalisation that exploits vulnerabilities, opaque dynamic pricing, subscription traps and customer service with no way to reach a human. A newsletter summary of the text says it singles out AI companions and conversational chatbots that simulate relationships to prolong use, and would ban variable-reward features for minors. Parliament and Council now negotiate.

What it means for GRC: Teams running consumer-facing chatbots, recommenders or pricing engines should add the proposal to their regulatory horizon. Human escalation paths and the logic behind personalisation look set to become audit points.

Source: European Commission →

7 · 🇺🇸 United States · 9 Nov 2026

NIST drafts adversarial ML taxonomy centred on attacks against AI agents

Consultation  GRC impact ■■■ Medium

NIST released the initial public draft of SP 2400-102e2026, a new edition of its adversarial machine learning taxonomy that now focuses on agentic systems. It sorts attacks by ML method, lifecycle stage and the attacker's goals, capabilities and knowledge, covering data and model poisoning, privacy attacks, evasion, prompt injection and agent hijacking, with matching mitigations. Comments are open until 18 Dec 2026.

What it means for GRC: Adopt its terms in AI threat models and red-team scopes so findings map to a shared vocabulary. Teams that test agent security should consider commenting before the deadline.

Source: NIST →
