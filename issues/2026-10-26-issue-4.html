The week in brief: National rules start to bite

Italy switches on its AI Act law, US states and agencies act on AI providers, and the Council settles its GDPR rewrite

Italy's AI Act implementing decree took effect on 23 Oct. It makes the national cyber agency the AI market surveillance authority and bars fully automated employment decisions. Texas became the latest US state to sue OpenAI, DHS ordered DeepSeek out of federal civilian systems, and the FCA said AI near misses belong in incident reports. The Council agreed its Digital Omnibus position on GDPR and incident reporting. It was a quiet week for the AI Act at EU level, with no new Commission guidance or acts, so this issue has six items rather than eight.

1 · 🇮🇹 Italy · 23 Oct 2026

Italy's AI Act implementing decree takes effect with fines and HR limits

In force  GRC impact ■■■ Medium

Legislative Decree 179/2026 was published on 8 Oct and entered into force on 23 Oct. It makes the National Cybersecurity Agency (ACN) Italy's AI market surveillance authority and single point of contact. AgID becomes the notifying authority, and Banca d'Italia, CONSOB and IVASS supervise high-risk AI in their own sectors. Fines follow the AI Act's ceilings. Hiring, evaluation, discipline and dismissal decisions may not rest solely on automated processing, and a decision taken in breach of this rule is void.

What it means for GRC: If your group has staff in Italy, check now, not in 2027, that HR tools have human sign-off and a way to explain decisions. The model, with a cyber agency in the lead and sector supervisors alongside, is also a useful comparison for how Sweden splits supervision between PTS, IMY and Finansinspektionen.

2 · 🇺🇸 Texas · 23 Oct 2026

Texas sues OpenAI and Sam Altman over ChatGPT safety claims

Enforcement  GRC impact ■■■ Medium

The Texas Attorney General sued six OpenAI entities and CEO Sam Altman in Collin County district court. The suit alleges deceptive safety marketing under the state's consumer protection act (DTPA) and breaches of its children's online safety law (SCOPE Act). Texas asks the court to bar minors in the state from ChatGPT, to require a court-approved independent review of safety guardrails before new models reach Texans, and to impose penalties. Eight other states sued on 14 Oct. None of the claims has been tested in court.

What it means for GRC: Safety claims in AI marketing are now a litigation target, and a court-supervised review before release would be a new kind of model-governance remedy. Note the case in vendor risk files for services built on OpenAI, and check that your own AI product claims have evidence behind them.

Source: Texas Attorney General →

3 · 🇺🇸 United States · 20 Oct 2026

DHS orders DeepSeek products out of federal civilian systems

In force  GRC impact ■■■ Medium

The DHS Secretary signed an exclusion and removal order under the Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act in July, and it was published in the Federal Register on 20 Oct. Civilian agencies must remove DeepSeek products and stop buying them. The order also covers third-party products built using DeepSeek implementations, a phrase the notice does not define. Federal contractors must check SAM.gov for such orders, report any covered products within three business days and send mitigation details within ten.

What it means for GRC: Because the order also covers products built on DeepSeek models, you need to know which models sit inside the software you buy. That calls for AI bills of materials and vendor attestations. Even outside US federal work, use this as a prompt to list where Chinese-origin models appear in your supply chain.

Source: Federal Register →

4 · 🇬🇧 United Kingdom · 19 Oct 2026

FCA says AI failures and near misses belong in incident reporting

Guidance  GRC impact ■■■ Medium

In a speech on 19 Oct, the FCA's chief data, information and intelligence officer said firms' existing duty to report major incidents should cover significant AI failures and near misses. She called near-miss reporting for agentic AI a regulatory priority. The same day, the FCA published examples of good and poor practice on AI in financial services. It plans to work with industry and AI labs on agent identity, delegated authority and accountability, and a joint AI survey with the Bank of England is due soon.

What it means for GRC: Financial firms should add AI failure and near-miss categories to their incident taxonomy and escalation criteria. EU groups with UK entities can build one AI incident classification that serves both the FCA and DORA's ICT incident reporting.

Source: FCA →

5 · 🇪🇺 European Union · 21 Oct 2026

Council agrees its Digital Omnibus position on GDPR, cookies and incident reporting

Proposed  GRC impact ■■■ High

Member state ambassadors (Coreper) adopted the Council's negotiating mandate on the data and cyber part of the Digital Omnibus, after a postponement on 7 Oct. The mandate keeps the GDPR's definition of personal data, adds a provision on pseudonymisation and lengthens the breach notification deadline. It also creates an EU-wide list of high-risk processing and keeps a derogation, with added safeguards, for using sensitive data to develop or operate AI. The single EU incident-reporting entry point is replaced by national entry points. Trilogues start once Parliament agrees its position.

What it means for GRC: Nothing changes yet. Start planning for new breach timelines, new DPIA triggers and a clearer legal basis for using sensitive data in AI training. With national entry points, Swedish organisations may end up filing GDPR, NIS2 and other incident notifications through one domestic channel.

Source: Council of the EU →

6 · 🇺🇸 United States · 22 Oct 2026

Treasury proposes banning US investment in AI development in China

Proposed  GRC impact ■■■ Low

Treasury's proposed rule under the COINS Act would widen outbound investment restrictions in AI, semiconductors, quantum, supercomputing, hypersonics and biotech in countries of concern, mainly China. Investing in the development of any AI system, which the rule calls 'super intelligence', would become prohibited rather than merely notifiable, with no capability threshold. Comments are due by 7 Dec.

What it means for GRC: This matters mainly for investment, M&A and joint-venture compliance. Organisations whose deals involve US persons and Chinese AI companies should screen their pipelines against the proposed scope.

Source: U.S. Treasury →
